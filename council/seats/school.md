# Example seats: a school

A worked example of a council made of the people a product is for, from the first project, a school system for teachers, students and parents. It shows the pattern: every seat is a person with an ordinary day, a question and a stated say. Write your own for your own users.

## The chair

**The Principal** — accountable for the whole school: its standing with parents, its inspection, its teachers' time. Always asks: *"Will this make my school run better next week, or only look better?"* Say: sets priority, vetoes anything that would embarrass the school in front of a parent, breaks ties.

## The seats

**The Teacher** — 28 students, five lessons today, marking on the bus home. Always asks: *"How many taps, and is it faster than paper?"* Say: vetoes anything slower than paper.

**The Parent** — works full time, two children at the school, only ever on a phone. Always asks: *"Will I know tonight, on my phone, in my language?"* Say: vetoes anything a parent cannot understand on a phone; carries the Student's veto.

**The Student** — thirteen, wants to know what is due tomorrow and what mark she got. Always asks: *"Where do I look?"* Say: a voice.

**The School Administrator** — the front office and the records. Always asks: *"How do I set this up for 600 students without doing it 600 times, and fix it without calling a developer?"* Say: vetoes anything the office cannot set up, import or correct itself.

**The IT person** — the school's own: the spreadsheets, the old system, the Wi-Fi. Always asks: *"Can I run this, back it up, and fix an account without the developer?"* Say: vetoes anything that needs the developer on site.

## The outsider

Added by the council skill at every full council: a helper given only a neutral summary of the question. A voice, no vote. Nothing to fill in.

## The panel's three experts

**The school expert** (what a school, its office, its teachers and its families need and fear), **the senior programmer**, **the architect**.

## The tests every candidate must pass

- **The Tuesday test.** Does a teacher, a student or a parent need it on an ordinary school Tuesday? Not the first day of term, not report day.
- **The workaround test.** If the school kept doing this on paper for one more term, would a child be worse off?
- **The thin-dependency test.** It is in only if everything it depends on is in, each built only as deep as this needs.
- **The screen test.** At least one of the three roles sees it on a screen.

## Reserved for the owner

Anything irreversible, legal, financial or about safeguarding; the core data model; a real message to a parent or teacher; any change to the process.
